Ask for public records

Under the Arkansas Freedom of Information Act (FOIA), any citizen of Arkansas can look at and get copies of City records, except those the law keeps private. You do not have to say why you want them.

Who to ask

  • Most City records: the City Clerk-Treasurer, Marla Vickery, at City Hall, (870) 555-0134.
  • Police reports and records: the Police Department records desk, 302 Front Street, (870) 555-0132.

How to ask

  • Ask in person, by phone, by mail, by email or with the form on this page. The form is optional.
  • A request does not have to be in writing, but a written request gives you and the City a record of what you asked for.
  • Describe the records clearly enough that staff can find them with reasonable effort, for example the topic, the dates and the names involved.
  • You can ask for copies in any format the City already has the records in, or can easily convert them to. The City does not have to create a new record to answer a request.

How long it takes

If the records are ready, you can see them right away. If they are in active use or in storage, the City tells you so in writing and sets a date and time, within 3 working days, when you can see them.

What it costs

  • Looking at records is free.
  • Copies cost no more than what it actually costs the City to make them, plus mailing or emailing. You get an itemized list of the charges.
  • If copies will cost more than $25, the City may ask you to pay first.

If you ask in writing and some records are exempt from the law, the City tells you in writing which exemptions apply.

Source: the Arkansas Attorney General's Freedom of Information Handbook, 22nd edition (2026).

Request form (optional)

You do not have to use this form. A call, a letter, an email or a visit to City Hall works the same way.

Be as specific as you can: the topic, dates, addresses or names
How would you like them?

Messages and reports you send the City may be public records under the Arkansas Freedom of Information Act. If someone asks for them, the City may have to share what you wrote, unless the law keeps it private.
