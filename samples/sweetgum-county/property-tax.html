Property taxes and assessment

The assessor sets your property's value. The quorum court, cities and school districts set the millage. The collector sends the bill and takes payment.

Key dates

January 1
Property is valued as of this date for the year.
January 1 to May 31
Assess your personal property. After May 31, a 10% late penalty is added.
March 1, 2027
The collector starts taking payment of 2026 taxes (the first business day of March).
October 15, 2027
2026 taxes are due. After October 15, a 10% penalty is added.

Dates from Arkansas law: taxes for a year are due October 15 of the next year (Ark. Code 26-36-201).

Why your bill can be different

  • Your taxable value can be lower than 20% of market value. Under Amendment 79 to the Arkansas Constitution, a homestead's taxable value can rise at most 5% a year, and other real estate's at most 10%, until it reaches full assessed value.
  • If you are 65 or older, or disabled, you can ask the assessor to freeze your home's taxable value.
  • Cars, trucks, boats and other personal property are billed on their own lines.
  • Some properties also owe charges the collector adds, such as an improvement district's.

Pay your taxes

Pay online, in person at the collector's office, Room 104, or by mail to the collector at the courthouse.

Late on your 2025 taxes? They were due October 15, 2026. Call the collector for the amount with the penalty: (870) 555-0154.

Homestead credit and freeze

The homestead credit takes up to $675 a year off the real estate tax on the home you live in. Apply once with the assessor; you can claim 1 homestead.

Owners 65 or older, or disabled, who have the credit can also apply with the assessor to freeze the home's taxable value.

Assess your personal property

Each year from January 1 to May 31, assess the cars, trucks, boats, trailers and ATVs you owned on January 1. After May 31, a 10% late penalty is added.

Assess by phone at (870) 555-0153, in person at Room 105, or online.

Real estate values

The assessor keeps the values of land and buildings. If you build, add on or tear something down, tell the assessor's office.

If you disagree with your value, talk with the assessor first. If you still disagree, you can appeal to the county equalization board.
